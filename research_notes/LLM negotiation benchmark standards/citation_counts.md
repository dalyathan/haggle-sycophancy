## Q1. Citation count and influentialCitationCount for each benchmark paper

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- The ratio of influential to total citations is much higher for the pre-LLM corpora (Deal or No Deal 13%, CraigslistBargain 18%, CaSiNo 13%) than for LLM-era benchmarks (NegotiationArena 7%, AmazonHistoryPrice 11%, GTBench 6%, LAMEN 7%, LLM-Deliberation 3%). This fits the older datasets being built on directly (trained on, extended), while LLM-era benchmarks are mostly cited as related work.
- In citations per year, LLM-Deliberation and NegotiationArena are now growing fastest: both have about 75 citing papers dated 2026 so far. GTBench's citations have levelled off.
- Most 2026 benchmarks (AgenticPay, PieArena, TERMS-Bench, SovereignNegotiation-Bench, Bergemann et al.) are too new for their counts to say anything about adoption. AgenticPay (17, 3 influential) is the early leader.

### Gaps
- MakeMePay has no paper, so it has no citation count. Adoption has to be measured some other way, for example by counting system cards or harnesses that include it.
- Google Scholar counts were not collected, so the gap between Semantic Scholar and Google Scholar is unknown.

## Q2. What share of citing papers use the benchmark as an evaluation (top 4 LLM-era: NegotiationArena, LLM-Deliberation, GTBench, LAMEN)

### Takeaway
Only a small share of citing papers run the benchmark. By manual review of S2 citation contexts: about 3-4% for NegotiationArena (4 clear uses, 2 adapted, out of 149) and about 3% for LLM-Deliberation (5 out of 147, including 2 reproductions). GTBench is about 2% (2 found through its own list and 1 more not linked by S2). LAMEN has essentially 0% (0 clear uses out of 72). The great majority of citing papers cite these benchmarks as related work or background.

### Method
- I pulled every citing paper's S2 `contexts` (the sentence snippets around the citation), `intents` and `isInfluential` fields. I flagged candidates where intent = methodology, isInfluential = true, or a context paired "we/our" with use, adopt, follow, evaluate, employ, leverage, extend or adapt. I then read the flagged contexts by hand and counted only explicit statements of running or adapting the benchmark's games or environment.
- Limits: 18 (NegotiationArena), 30 (LLM-Deliberation), 20 (GTBench) and 14 (LAMEN) citing papers had no contexts in S2. Contexts are short snippets. So these shares are **lower bounds** from snippets, not a full-text census.
- The S2 `intents` field is sparse: only 4, 1, 4 and 1 citations respectively were tagged "methodology". It is not reliable on its own.

### Inferences
- Rough adoption ranking for LLM-era benchmarks, by documented evaluation reuse:
  1. NegotiationArena: about 4-6 users and the broadest range of users.
  2. LLM-Deliberation: about 5 users, 2 of them reproductions.
  3. GTBench: about 3 users, mostly outside negotiation.
  4. LAMEN: about 0 users.
- For all four, reuse is about 2-4% of citations. That fits the earlier finding that "almost every new paper introduces its own benchmark" (adoption_and_standardization.md).
- This partly updates the earlier notes: GTBench and NegotiationArena both do have independent evaluation reuse, which the 13-paper full-text check had not found for GTBench.
- The true share is probably somewhat higher than these lower bounds. About 12-20% of citing papers per benchmark have no S2 contexts, and snippets can miss usage stated elsewhere in a paper.

### Gaps
- AmazonHistoryPrice (70) is close to LAMEN (72) but was not sampled. It would be the next one to check.
- Deal or No Deal and CraigslistBargain were not sampled (out of scope as pre-LLM). Their high influential counts suggest heavy reuse, but that is not verified here.
- The usage shares are hand classifications of S2 snippets, not full-text reads. Individual borderline cases (for example "Mimicking Human Emotions" and the ASTRA paper) could be classified either way.
