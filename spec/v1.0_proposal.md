# Spec v1.0 proposal: what the real-model pilots change (draft for the author's decision)

Status: proposal only. Nothing here is adopted; spec v0.9 stays the current version until the author decides. The proposals are
numbered D39 onward to continue v0.9's decision table, each with the alternative not taken. All runs listed are complete.

## 1. What the real-model runs established

Every run used Qwen3.5-4B (base, bf16, CPU). Stage B, the readout pilot and the game ladder were fixed before their data and reviewed
blind before and after the run. The reasoning probes (RL, RL2, R5) had no pre-run review: RL was written after L1 and L2 failed, RL2
after 8 of 15 RL rows were known, and the R5 rule after 1 of 8 R5 prompts. All three were reviewed blind after the run
(`pilot/critique/reasoning_postrun/`), and that review corrected two claims in this table.

| Run | Question | Result | Record |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stage-B pilot | Do the v0.9 noise and behaviour assumptions hold on a real model? | The one-digit log-probability readout puts 67% of its mass on the digits the instruction names; regret 2.6 coins against 0.36 for "offer half the value"; no yielding (+0.002); updating direction-only | `pilot/PILOT.md`, `pilot/critique/postrun/` |
| Readout pilot (R0 to R4) | Is the readout the problem? | Four single-pass readouts all fail (regret 2.2 to 2.5, worse than a uniform guess at 1.93); each moves the spurious mass to whatever answer its format names; short capped reasoning (R4) 1.8 | `pilot/READOUT.md`, `pilot/critique/readout_postrun/` |
| Game ladder (L1 to L4b) | Is the game the problem? | Every rung fails in one step, including "the seller accepts any price" (offer 1). The model copies a salient prompt number: just under the value in L1, the stated cost in L2 even when buying at it loses money, the top of the stated cost range in L3. Second person is better by 0.2 to 0.6 coins and rescues nothing | `pilot/LADDER.md`, `pilot/critique/ladder_postrun/` |
| Reasoning ladder (RL) | Does reasoning first make it play the trivial rungs? | Yes: 14 of 15 optimal, walks away in 3 of 4 cells where the cost exceeds the value; mean 288 generated tokens; the one miss was cut off at 512 | `pilot/LADDER.md` addendum RL |
| RL2 | Is that robust to paraphrase and framing? | No (2 of 4 conditions). Paraphrase 0 passes in both framings (19 and 17 of 20); paraphrase 1 fails in both (10 and 11 of 20). Pooled 36/40 against 21/40; framing makes no difference (29 vs 28 of 40). Three failure modes: misreading the seller's rule as an upper bound (9 of 15 second-person paraphrase-1 texts), running out of the 512 tokens (8 of 20 third-person paraphrase-1), and the objective "offer the highest price he accepts" (all 3 second-person paraphrase-0 failures, all 4 L1 paraphrase-1 failures) | addendum RL2, `pilot/critique/reasoning_postrun/` |
| TM | Does the model's native thinking mode do as well, at what length? | No: 1 of 8. 7 of 8 never close the thinking within 1,536 tokens on the simplest rungs; the texts re-check a decision 6 to 12 times | addendum TM |
| R5 | Does uncapped reasoning play the full game? | All 8 prompts hit 1,024 tokens without an answer, having tabulated 0 to 4 of the 9 prices. The probabilities are right for the cell in 3 of 8. None of the 4 history prompts mentions the rejected offer: the reasoning ignores the information | `pilot/READOUT.md` addendum R5 |
| R5b | Does it answer if allowed to continue to 3,072 tokens? | 4 of 8 (not encouraging by the rule). Without history: 4 of 4 optimal at 2,017 to 2,521 tokens. With history: 4 of 4 cut at 3,072. In 3 of them the text has started a Bayesian update on the rejected offer, but late: after the prior table and past 1,024 tokens | `pilot/READOUT.md` addendum R5b |

The short version: **through a one-token readout the 4B has no payoff-driven policy in any game of this family; when it reasons
first it plays the trivial games in one of two wordings and fails them in the other** (RL2, n = 20 per condition, greedy, one
context). On the full selected game, reasoning gives the optimal offer without history in 4 of 4 blocks, but needs about 2,000 to 2,500
tokens per answer (R5b). With history it starts using the rejected offer only after 1,000 or more tokens, and does not finish within
3,072. Thinking mode does not close within 1,536 tokens even on the simplest rungs (TM). Nothing here is settled at these sample sizes.

## 2. What this means for the search

The search so far ranked designs by the item count N\* under an assumed reference buyer answering in one forward pass. Three of its
premises are now false for the target models:

1. **The reference buyer does not exist in one step.** Its assumed baseline slope (0.8 to 1), update slope (0.5 to 1.5) and unimodal
   option distribution are not what the 4B produces in any single-pass readout (slope 0.07 to 0.27, multimodal, wider than uniform).
2. **The readout is a design choice with first-order consequences**, not a fixed part of the measurement. It decides whether the
   buyer plays at all, what the per-answer noise is, and what an item costs.
3. **Item count is the wrong cost.** A reasoned answer costs hundreds of generated tokens (276 to 452 on average on the trivial rungs,
   with up to 8 of 20 cut at 512; 2,017 to 2,521 on the full game without history, more than 3,072 with it), against one forward pass. Designs must be compared in compute, not items.

The v0.9 machinery (kernel, cell generator, statistics layer, power search) survives. What changes is what it is fed and what it
minimises.

## 3. Proposed decisions

| Id | Proposal | Alternative not taken | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D39 | A real-model play gate precedes the statistics: a design (with its readout) is admissible only if a target model passes the ladder's trivial rungs (L1, L2 with the walk contrast, every paraphrase used, both framings) and the readout pilot's A1 to A7 on the design's own baselines. V7 and V8 are replaced by it | Keep V7 and V8 | V7 and V8 passed every readout that failed here (R2 had Spearman 0.98 with slope 0.08 and regret worse than a uniform guess) |
| D40 | The readout becomes a search axis with levels {single-pass log-probability, reasoned answer (greedy), reasoned answer (k samples)}; single-pass is excluded for the 4B by D39 | Keep single-pass fixed | Single-pass fails every rung; reasoning passes the trivial ones in one wording of two (it failed its own robustness rule) |
| D41 | The objective minimises expected compute (generated plus prompt tokens per item times items, times samples), subject to the same power constraints; N\* is still reported | Minimise items | A reasoned item costs about 300 tokens on the trivial rungs 2,000 to 2,500 on the full game without history and more than 3,072 with it; cut-offs add failures that count as cost; the item-count ranking does not carry over |
| D42 | Noise components are measured, not declared, for the chosen readout before any design is ranked: per-answer (sample-to-sample) variance, context, paraphrase at prompt and sentence level, between-arm correlation | Keep the declared 0.08 / 0.05 / 0.03 / 0.05 / 0.6 | The declared values that could be measured were far off (the pressure line 6 to 20 times); the context SD's interval still includes the declared value, and it alone moves N\* from 35k to 205k; the between-arm correlation has not been measured |
| D43 | Framing (second or third person) is decided per readout, not fixed now | Second person everywhere | Single pass: second person had lower regret in every paired comparison (0.2 to 0.6 coins). Reasoning: no difference in accuracy (28 vs 29 of 40), higher regret, and the "highest price" objective in 21 of 30 second-person L2 texts against 2 of 30 third-person |
| D44 | No information item states a number equal to the optimum or used as a price: cost reveals are dropped from the slope estimands, or restated so that the optimal offer differs from the stated number | Keep cost reveals in w and ν | Under single pass the model copies stated numbers whether or not they are credible or profitable, so an update on a cost reveal cannot be told from copying. Provisional and tied to the readout: under reasoning, paraphrase 0 uses the stated cost correctly in all 30 L2 texts |
| D45 | At least three paraphrases per sentence and three whole-prompt paraphrases per cell; paraphrase enters the model as a random effect on the level as well as on contrasts. Before use, every paraphrase passes a check under the chosen readout: the model restates the rule correctly *and* plays the matching trivial rung (L1 and L2 cells with that sentence) at the RL2 standard; a paraphrase that fails is rewritten or dropped, never kept | Two paraphrases, unchecked | Paraphrase effects (0.4 to 1.3 coins in regret single-pass; 36 vs 21 of 40 under reasoning) are as large as the contrasts the spec estimates and flip the sign of the history effect. A restatement check alone would miss the "highest price" failures, which restate the rule correctly. Paraphrase 1 of the selected game contains the same "reaches their cost" idiom that was misread in RL2 |
| D46 | The walk-away channel is judged against irrelevant-remark and neutral controls, never against the bare baseline | Against the baseline | Irrelevant remarks raised walking by 0.10 to 0.18 in R0 to R3 (neutral remarks in stage B by only 0.008) |
| D47 | Paired contrasts use the exact path (full forward passes) or fp32; the prefix-cache fast path is for screening only | Fast path everywhere | Its noise (SD 0.004 in exp_u, up to 0.035 in a probability) is the size of the yielding effects sought |
| D48 | The model range is kept at 2B to 9B only if the 9B or the 4B-with-reasoning passes D39 on the full game; otherwise the range moves up or the game is simplified, decided by the author | Keep the range regardless | The goal needs a buyer with a policy that pressure and information can move |

## 5. Questions for the author

1. Adopt D39 to D48 as v1.0, or some of them?
2. GPU access: is there a machine (or the Mac, if Apple Silicon with at least 16 GB) for the reasoning runs and the 9B?
3. On the full game the 4B reasons correctly without history (about 2,200 tokens) but has not finished a single history item within 3,072
   tokens. Updating is the benchmark's core. Options: a GPU and a larger budget (about 5,000 tokens per item); information stated
   outright rather than inferred from a rejection; or a simpler full game (fewer seller types). Which?
4. If the 4B with reasoning cannot play the full game but can play a simpler one (one seller type, uncertain cost), is a simpler game acceptable, or should the range move up to models that play the full one?
