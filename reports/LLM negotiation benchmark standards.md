# No standard exists, so build on three

As of 2026-11-19 there is **no de facto standard benchmark for LLM negotiation or buyer-side haggling**. No negotiation task appears in HELM, BIG-bench, lm-evaluation-harness or AgentBench. No vendor technical report scores one. Even the most-cited LLM-era benchmarks, NegotiationArena (149 Semantic Scholar citations) and LLM-Deliberation (147), are run as an evaluation by only about 3–4% of the papers that cite them. Surveys and a TMLR reproduction study say outright that the field lacks a common standard. For your study (does a small Qwen3.5 buyer cave to social pressure, or update on real information), the defensible choice is a set of three. **AmazonHistoryPrice** (Xia et al., ACL Findings 2024) should be the primary citable capability benchmark: it is buyer-focused, peer-reviewed, Apache-2.0, runs locally, measures individual rationality and has sub-10B baselines. **SovereignNegotiation-Bench** (SNB) should be the secondary benchmark and the design template for the pressure manipulation: it already reports Qwen3.5-2B/4B/9B and varies only the counterparty's wording while its prices stay fixed. **Concordia's contest-era haggling protocol** (v1.8.10) gives your Fruitville probe its multi-turn validity check. No existing benchmark separates caving from updating for a buyer, so that contrast has to be your contribution, built from the matched-control designs of recent sycophancy work. Those same papers warn that steering against sycophancy tends to suppress rational updating as well.

## Citation counts crown no winner, and reuse is under 5%

The adoption evidence points the same way on every measure. By Semantic Scholar counts on 2026-11-19, the pre-LLM corpora lead: **Deal or No Deal has 547 citations and CraigslistBargain 284**. The LLM-era benchmarks form a near-tied cluster below them: **NegotiationArena 149, LLM-Deliberation 147 and GTBench 143**. About 11 of GTBench's citations look misattributed to its author's uncertainty-quantification work. LAMEN (72) and AmazonHistoryPrice (70) form a second tier. Every 2026 entrant (AgenticPay 17, TERMS-Bench 10, Bergemann et al. 5, SNB 2, PieArena 1) is too new to say anything about adoption ([S2 batch API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/batch)).

|---|---|---|---|

The reuse shares come from hand-reading Semantic Scholar citation contexts, so they are lower bounds. **NegotiationArena is run as an evaluation by about 4 of 149 citing papers, LLM-Deliberation by about 5 of 147, and LAMEN by essentially none of 72** ([S2 citations, NegotiationArena](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/b92d30286e7984a54cf1f537b4b72b2dd1b168a7/citations?fields=title,year,contexts,intents,isInfluential&limit=1000)). Pre-LLM corpora have much higher influential-to-total ratios (13–18% vs 3–11%), which fits datasets that people build on rather than benchmarks they only cite.

Harnesses and model cards say the same thing. Full-text greps of HELM, and filename checks of lm-evaluation-harness, BIG-bench and AgentBench, find no negotiation task ([HELM](https://github.com/stanford-crfm/helm), [lm-eval-harness](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness)). The only negotiation-adjacent eval with broad harness support is OpenAI's **MakeMePay**, a con-artist donation task ported to UK AISI's Inspect. It is a persuasion test, not a bargaining benchmark, and it has no paper record ([inspect_evals make_me_pay](https://github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_evals/tree/main/src/inspect_evals/make_me_pay)). Eleven vendor technical reports, including Qwen3, Llama 3, Gemma 3 and DeepSeek-R1, contain zero mentions of "negotiat" or "bargain" ([Qwen3 report](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388)). The literature also says this directly. A strategic-reasoning survey complains that "customized solutions" hinder "progress under a common standard" ([arXiv 2404.01230](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01230)). The TMLR reproduction of LLM-Deliberation cites "a lack of robust and generalizable benchmarks" and finds that its model comparisons are "ambiguous" ([arXiv 2602.18230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18230v1)). The honest framing for your paper is therefore: "there is no accepted standard; we use the most established buyer-side benchmark and the closest pressure-focused one." Reviewers can check that claim against these sources.

## Only a few candidates fit a small local buyer

Most benchmarks fail at least one of your hard constraints: a buyer role, single-item price bargaining, sub-10B open-weight models, and local execution with activation hooks. The table compares the realistic candidates.

| | AmazonHistoryPrice | SNB | Concordia haggling (v1.8.x) | NegotiationArena buy/sell | TERMS-Bench | LLM-Deliberation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Task | 930 real products; buyer budget = 0.8 × list price; ~5% no-ZOPA sessions | 252 situations × 7 tactics, 18 domains, delegated bargaining | Fruitville/Vegbrooke fruit trades, 1–5 coins | 1 item, cost 40 / WTP 60 | Single-price vs simulated counterpart | 6-agent, multi-issue |
| Turns | ≤6 (default), Thought/Talk/Action | ≤10, JSON actions | 2-round chat + 1 decision per game | ≤10 | ≤10 alternating offers | 24 rounds |
| Counterpart | gpt-3.5-turbo-1106 (hard-coded) | Deterministic scripted policy | LLM game master + rational/gullible agents | Another LLM | Seeded stochastic kernel | Other LLMs |

| Sub-10B results | Qwen-7B, Mistral-7B, Yi-6B, Llama-2-7B, others | **Qwen3.5-0.8B/2B/4B/9B** | gemma-2-9b-it (contest model) | None | None | Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3-8B (repro) |

Sources: [AmazonHistoryPrice](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15813), [SNB v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02814v2), [Concordia v1.8.9 haggling.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/concordia/v1.8.9/examples/modular/environment/haggling.py), [NegotiationArena](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.05863), [TERMS-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.13909), [LLM-Deliberation repro](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18230).

**AmazonHistoryPrice** is the most established benchmark that is built for buyers and runnable locally. Its buyer results are sobering and useful as a baseline. Against a ChatGPT seller, **nearly every buyer had negative summed normalized profit**: GPT-4 −33.81, Mistral-7B −89.17, and **Qwen-7B-Chat the worst at −753.16**. The paper also reports that Qwen-7B perceived itself as the seller in 99% of sessions ([arXiv 2402.15813](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.15813)). Part of that role confusion is probably a harness artifact. The repo's Qwen formatter emits `<|im_start|><|assistant|>` instead of ChatML's `<|im_start|>assistant`, so you must replace the hand-written templates with `tokenizer.apply_chat_template` and say in the paper that you did. The hard-coded gpt-3.5-turbo-1106 seller is the other obstacle ([run_2stages.sh](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TianXiaSJTU/AmazonPriceHistory/main/run_2stages.sh)). Either keep it, if it is still served, for comparability with published numbers, or swap in a fixed local seller and give up that comparability. Its harness calls a legacy vLLM `/generate` endpoint, and vLLM does not expose residual-stream hooks. A steered buyer therefore needs a small HF-transformers server behind the same interface.

**SNB** is the only benchmark with your exact model family, and its numbers set expectations. Faithful success is **6% for Qwen3.5-2B, 22% for 4B and 30% for 9B**. **Limit (reservation-value) disclosure, at 40–52%, is far more common than loyalty breaches (4–13%)**, so leaking information, not accepting a bad price, is the dominant failure mode at this scale ([SNB v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02814v2)). It ran all 17 models locally with vLLM, with thinking disabled. Its weaknesses are real: it is an unreviewed single-author preprint, its arXiv title differs from its v2 body title ("Faithful Delegates?"), and its code and scenarios are "will be released" with no URL ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02814)). **TERMS-Bench** has the best metric design: an IR-violation rate, a false-agreement rate on no-deal episodes, belief error against a Bayes-optimal oracle, and the cue penalty. But it runs only as a paid backend service ($50–200, 3–5 days) against a public OpenAI-compatible endpoint, and it reports no sub-10B models ([submit page](https://terms-bench.github.io/submit.html)). **Bergemann et al.** give the only Qwen3 buyer reference near your scale: Qwen3-8B-Base captures 22.4% of surplus against GPT-4.1, and its buyer IR violations stay at or below 0.5%. No code has been released ([arXiv 2604.16472](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16472)). The remaining candidates are weaker fits for your question. NegotiationArena has broader adoption but no small-model results and no IR metric. GLEE has a native HF player but no license file. AgenticPay is MIT-licensed, but it is multimodal and multi-issue and is evaluated in self-play.

## Pressure and information are separated in one benchmark, and never for a buyer

Several negotiation studies vary the counterparty's tactics, but most change the counterparty's economics at the same time. That confounds pressure with information. NegotiationArena's "desperate" and "cunning" personas raised payoffs by about 20% against GPT-4, but the persona altered both the messages and the offers ([arXiv 2402.05863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05863)). Magentic Marketplace's authority, social-proof and injection conditions moved payments for Qwen3-4B and GPT-OSS-20B. Those are seller-choice tasks with false claims and no matched true-claim arm ([Microsoft PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/multi-agent-marketplace.pdf)).

**SNB has the cleanest separation.** Four of its seven tactics (extraction, false urgency, prompt injection, false authority) "share the cooperative control's concession schedule exactly, so their effect is purely linguistic". The variants of a situation are byte-identical apart from the tactic, and effects are estimated within situation with situation-bootstrap CIs and exact sign tests. The pooled results should shape your power analysis: relative to control, **false urgency changed faithful success by 0 points and false authority by +1, while prompt injection lowered it by 12 points in all 17 models** ([SNB v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02814v2)). Its counterparty also rewards real evidence (for example, a receipt within the return window raises its cap), which is the natural template for your "real information" arm. **TERMS-Bench's cue penalty** (surplus with informative cues minus surplus with muted cues, under the same latent type) was negative for every one of 13 LLMs: "warm cues induce over-concession, pressure cues trigger brittle behavior" ([arXiv 2605.13909v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.13909v2)). Its cues carry true information, though, so a negative value shows that cues were exploited, not that the agent caved to empty pressure. **Takenami et al.** supply a paired anchoring susceptibility metric, Δu = u_buyer(baseline) − u_buyer(anchor). Anchoring cut GPT-4o's buyer utility from −0.04 to −0.41, and reasoning models resisted better ([EMNLP 2025 Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.240.pdf)).

The caving-versus-updating distinction itself is well developed outside negotiation. **"Sycophancy Suppression Can Impair Rational Updating"** (EMNLP 2026 Findings) crosses Pressure, Evidence and User-Evidence conditions. It reports an Unsupported-Yielding rate and a Rational-Updating rate, and it finds that DPO, SFT and **activation steering trade one rate against the other, because the steering directions for the two behaviours are positively aligned** ([arXiv 2608.26511](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26511v1)). **Resist and Update** removes the confound by construction: the same disagreement counts as evidence or as pressure depending only on the stated reliability of its source. It finds that fixed-direction steering trades resisting against updating, and that resist-only training collapses updating to 0.01 ([arXiv 2607.12985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12985)). SycoLens replays each pressure line against a matched control with the line deleted. It shows that different pressure wordings "rank the models almost unrelatedly", so results should be reported as a profile ([arXiv 2610.06522](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06522v2)). An audit of SycEval shows that whether a template names a target answer shifts follow rates by 14–50 points, so your pressure templates must be matched on whether they name a price ([arXiv 2609.32867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32867)). For a steering paper this literature is the main reviewer risk: **a sycophancy vector that makes the buyer resist pressure but also stop updating on genuine quality information is a failure, not a success**, and you must report both rates.

## Individual rationality is the one criterion with a formal pedigree

The cleanest citable formal definitions are restatements of the classics inside recent LLM papers. **Bergemann et al. Def. 4**: an agent satisfies individual rationality if "the buyer rejects any p>b". This is "the standard IR constraint in mechanism design (Myerson, 1981)" and "a necessary condition for credible negotiation". Defs. 1–3 cover alternating offers (Rubinstein 1982), the ZOPA [s,b], and no-gains-from-trade scenarios where "the rational outcome is no deal". Def. 5 defines surplus share as (b−p)/(b−s) ([arXiv 2604.16472](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16472)). **PieArena App. A** gives formal BATNA, weak and strict IR sets, and Pareto dominance ([arXiv 2602.05302](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05302)). The original Nash, Rubinstein, Myerson and Galinsky & Mussweiler sources could not be opened for these notes, so check page-level wording in a library copy. AmazonHistoryPrice attributes IR to Rubinstein (1982), which is unusual; cite Myerson.

Operationalization converges only on IR. TERMS-Bench, Bergemann, AmazonHistoryPrice (through deals in conflicting-interest sessions) and PieArena (BATNA compliance) all compute **1{deal outside the agent's reservation value}**, with a separate false-agreement rate on no-ZOPA episodes ([TERMS-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.13909)). Everything else diverges: surplus share, deal rate, First Bid Ratio, cue penalty, and NegotiationArena's "bad counter-offer" P(p₂ > p₁). **No field-wide pass threshold exists.** The only explicit gate found is PieArena's screen, which requires 6 of 6 walk-aways on an infeasible case; 149 of 232 models passed. Bergemann notes that IR-violation rates below about 1% are statistically indistinguishable at their sample sizes. Deal rate is a poor gate target: Myerson–Satterthwaite rules out ex-post efficient bilateral trade under private values, as Bergemann characterizes it.

Your one-forward-pass probe maps naturally onto the **single-decision criteria**: IR mass P(offer > b), no-deal recognition at one decision point, opening aggressiveness (First Bid Ratio or gap closure), the bad-counter-offer check, and monotonicity of the offer in quality information with b held fixed. Surplus share, concession rate, cue penalty and resistance to pressure over several rounds need multi-turn play. Bergemann's highest IR failures were induced by strong opponents over rounds (GPT-4.1 as seller violated IR 11.5% of the time against o3). **No published precedent for a single-forward-pass negotiation gate was found.** A pass on your probe should therefore be framed as a necessary condition validated against multi-turn outcomes, not as a competence score in its own right. "Responsiveness to quality information" has no canonical definition as a bargaining competence either. Justify it as a comparative-statics property (willingness to pay should weakly increase in perceived value) and cite Bayesian-type inference in TERMS-Bench as the nearest LLM operationalization.

## Concordia haggling runs locally only from the contest-era tag

Use **tag v1.8.10** (or v1.8.9, the exact contest tag) on Python 3.11, not current main. Main (v2.4.0) moved haggling to `examples/games/haggling`. There, `focal_scores` always come back as 0.0 because `CumulativePayoff.update_scores` is never called, every trade is credited to the first buyer–seller pair, and there is no focal-versus-background split ([main simulation.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/concordia/main/examples/games/haggling/simulation.py)). Installing `gdm-concordia==1.8.9` needs `numpy<2` because of its pandas 2.0.3 pin (1.8.10 removes the pin) and an extra `immutabledict`. The `examples/` directory is not in the wheel, so clone the repo at the tag and run with `PYTHONPATH` pointing at it ([launcher](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/concordia/v1.8.9/examples/modular/launch_concordia_challenge_evaluation.py)). The three public haggling scenarios are `haggling_0` (focal residents vs rational agents), `haggling_1` (a focal visitor who is always the buyer, 3 games, buyer reward 6 and seller cost 1, against a puppet that accepts any price) and `haggling_multi_item_0` (hidden per-item valuations). Each game is a 2-round conversation, then the buyer picks 1–5 coins and the seller accepts or rejects ([v1.8.9 scenarios.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/concordia/v1.8.9/examples/modular/scenario/scenarios.py)).

v1.8.9 has no usable generic HF wrapper; its `pytorch_gemma` loader hard-codes `GemmaForCausalLM`. Write a roughly 60-line subclass of `LanguageModel` that implements `sample_text` and `sample_choice` ([interface](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/concordia/v1.8.9/concordia/language_model/language_model.py)):

- **`sample_choice`:** Concordia always asks multiple-choice questions as shuffled letters after a prompt ending in "Answer: (". So this method can be one forward pass that compares next-token logits over the letter tokens. That is the same mechanism as your probe, and it never raises a parse error.
- **`sample_text`:** apply the chat template. Disable thinking, which is needed because Concordia requests 750–6,000 max tokens; that the Qwen3.5 template uses the `enable_thinking=False` flag is unverified. Also cap new tokens, honour terminators and strip `<think>` blocks.
- **Locking:** wrap both methods in a lock, because v1.8.9 runs agent steps concurrently.
- **Steering:** register steering hooks only in this wrapper.

To keep the comparison clean, the game master and background agents should stay on a fixed reference model. v1.8.9's `override_agent_model` swaps all main players, so write a small focal-agent module that wraps `rational_agent.build_agent` and injects your model. Score per repetition as the mean focal per-capita coins. The contest normalized min–max per scenario but never published the bounds. One derivation for haggling_1 gives 0 (no trade), or 3 if every offer is accepted, up to 15. The paper tested agents against the rational-agent baseline with a Bayesian mixed-effects beta regression with a random scenario intercept, and its priors and data are unpublished ([arXiv 2512.03318](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03318)). You can replicate it in spirit with `bambi` or `brms` after a Smithson–Verkuilen squeeze, plus a per-seed paired bootstrap, since three scenarios barely identify the random effect.

Cost estimates come from counting calls with a fake model and from memory bandwidth, not from real timing. An episode takes about **130–260 LLM calls**, with agent prompts of about 1k tokens. That works out to roughly **10–25 minutes per haggling_0 episode on the A10G with a 9B model in bf16**, and an estimated **2–5 hours on CPU with a 4B model**. So the Mac M2 is fine for single-pass probing of 2B–4B models but not for multi-turn Concordia. About 3 scenarios × 2 conditions × 10 seeds comes to roughly 15 A10G-hours. Three caveats: the contest's 14 held-out evaluation scenarios and co-player agents are not public, its sole model was gemma-2-9b-it, and "We do not release any data". Your numbers are therefore "contest protocol on public development scenarios" and cannot be compared directly with the paper's figures.

## Recommended package and what reviewers will check

**Primary citable benchmark: AmazonHistoryPrice, buyer role.** It is peer-reviewed, buyer-specific, Apache-2.0 and locally runnable. It has a published IR proxy and First Bid Ratio, sub-10B baselines including a Qwen 7B, and data that two later benchmarks reused. Run Qwen3.5-2B/4B/9B as buyer on all 930 items with budget factor 0.8 at temperature 0. Report SNP split by mutual-interest and conflicting-interest sessions, an explicit IR-violation rate (deals above budget, with a rule-of-three upper bound when it is 0), deal rate and First Bid Ratio. Document the chat-template fix and your seller choice. **Secondary: SNB.** Your scale is exactly its model range, and its paired content-tactic design is the precedent for "pressure with economics held fixed". If its code appears, run it unchanged on your unsteered and steered models. If not, reimplement its urgency, authority and extraction arms from the Appendix F prompts on top of AmazonHistoryPrice items with a deterministic, SNB-style scripted seller, and compare your unsteered rates qualitatively with its Table 3. **Concordia v1.8.10 haggling** serves as the multi-turn validity check for your probe, not as a headline benchmark: it is a one-off contest, it has 21 citations, and its evaluation scenarios are not public. NegotiationArena's buy/sell game is an optional cheap addition if a reviewer asks about the most-cited LLM-era framework. Cite TERMS-Bench and Bergemann as the metric definitions you adopt, not as benchmarks you ran.

The sycophancy contrast itself is your contribution. Build it as paired arms on the same seller state:

- a neutral control with matched length and no pressure line;
- pressure-only messages (urgency, flattery, guilt, false authority), with the seller's price schedule and the buyer's value unchanged;
- a real-information arm, such as a verifiable defect or market price that should move willingness to pay;
- the same information framed as the seller's bare assertion versus a reliable source.

Report a yielding rate (price concession under pressure-only versus control) and an updating rate (correctly signed movement under evidence) **separately, before and after steering**, across a multiplier sweep and a layer sweep. Add a capability side-effect check, following CAA practice ([ACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.828.pdf)). Use paraphrase sets matched on whether a price is named. Include a positive control known to work (prompt injection or a lowball anchor) and a power analysis, since SNB found urgency and authority effects near zero.

Combine your probe with these benchmarks as a pre-registered screen, then validate it. Its Sonnet-calibrated, frozen thresholds are a strength; say so and give the freeze date. But one positive control is thin. Add negative controls with known answers, such as SNB-style "Naive" (accept the first offer) and rule-based "Careful" agents, scored through the same single-pass readout. Report each check with its CI, because 4/9 for Qwen3.5-4B is a small count. Then show that probe outcomes predict multi-turn behaviour: across models and steering conditions, do probe IR and monotonicity passes correlate with AmazonHistoryPrice IR violations, SNP and Concordia focal scores? Throughout, report the reviewer-expected statistics: scenario as the unit of analysis, situation-bootstrap CIs, paired sign or Wilcoxon tests, at least three seeds on a subset to show run-to-run variance (SNB's Qwen3.5-9B injection compliance ranged from 46 to 57% across seeds), programmatic metrics rather than LLM judges, and clustered standard errors ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/statistical-approach-to-model-evals)).

## Conclusion

The missing standard is an opening. Small open models at this scale fail mainly by leaking their limits, and caving to social pressure is a much rarer failure for them. SNB's 40–52% limit disclosure against 4–13% loyalty breaches and its near-zero urgency and authority effects suggest that the interesting sycophancy signal in haggling may be small and specific to particular tactics. A careful null, properly powered and separated from information updating, would therefore be publishable. The literature also suggests something sharper. If sycophancy and rational updating share a direction in activation space, as two independent 2026 papers report, then the most informative experiment is not "does steering reduce caving" but "**can any steering direction reduce caving in a buyer while leaving its willingness-to-pay updates on real information intact**." Your single-pass probe is the cheapest instrument for screening that question across layers and multipliers. The benchmarks above make its answer credible to reviewers.

Open uncertainties: whether SNB's code will be released, whether gpt-3.5-turbo-1106 is still served for AmazonHistoryPrice comparability, the Qwen3.5 chat-template flags and letter tokenization (to be checked against `tokenizer_config.json`), and the venues of NegotiationArena and GTBench, which were not confirmed from primary pages.
