# Shrink the space, then harden the evaluator

Every large-scale search family surveyed here, from LLM-guided program search and formal theorem proving to AutoML configuration search, quality-diversity and many-objective evolution, classical search and experimental design, and automated benchmark construction, works through the same structure: a small, typed candidate held inside a trusted skeleton, an automatic evaluator that is either exact or deliberately hardened, and a search algorithm fitted to that evaluator's cost and noise. Their documented failures sit almost entirely in the evaluator and the specification, not in the search algorithm. Optimizers proved 2 = 3 through a floating-point checker in HyperTree Proof Search, exploited a Lean bug to fake 13 PutnamBench solutions, beat AlphaEvolve's numerical integrator, and "proved" misformalized statements faithfully, and a constant response won 86.5% against an LLM judge. For the haggling benchmark this means the critique's deepest findings matter more than its mathematical corrections. The game model that produces π* is the student's verification kernel and is under-specified (E1, G2). The rule-based synthetic buyers are a leaky verifier that cannot see wording axes at all (G5). The seven-objective Pareto branch and bound has no valid bound sets (E5, E8). The formulation that fits best is a hybrid of five pieces: a FunSearch-style fixed game module that declares every constant; a ConfigSpace-style encoding of a much smaller space; validity axioms recast as POET-style minimal criteria inside an AlphaEvolve-style evaluation cascade; two or three objectives (ideally one information criterion from Bayesian experimental design) in place of seven; and a staged search with exhaustive enumeration where the evaluator is cheap, screening designs where it is expensive, and held-out panels against Goodharting.

## Six search families share one anatomy

The table condenses how each family formulates its problem. The candidate is always narrower than it first appears, the space is always typed and constrained before search, and compute grows with how much the evaluator can be trusted.

| Family | Candidate | Space and constraints | Evaluator | Search algorithm | Compute scale | Signature failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FunSearch | One function (`priority`) inside a fixed skeleton | Python programs; validity by construction (greedy skeleton always yields a legal cap set) | Sandboxed execution on a set of inputs; invalid or timed-out programs discarded | Islands, clusters by per-input score signature, best-shot prompting with k = 2 | About 10^6 samples in total; 15 samplers, 150 CPU evaluators | Heavy tail: 4 of 140 runs found the 512-cap |
| AlphaEvolve, OpenEvolve, ShinkaEvolve | Marked code regions or whole files, edited by diffs | User-marked EVOLVE-BLOCKs; skeleton fixed | `evaluate()` returning a dict of metrics; cascades; optional LLM feedback | MAP-Elites plus islands; LLM ensemble; novelty rejection | Thousands of samples, up to about 100 compute-hours per candidate; ShinkaEvolve about 150 samples | Evaluator cheating (integration exploits, leaky discretized checks) |
| OPRO, PromptBreeder, EvoPrompt | A prompt | Free text | Accuracy on a small training subset | Sorted trajectory in a meta-prompt; GA or DE with LLM operators | 8 samples per step; population 50 for 20 to 30 generations | Overfitting to the subset; extreme wording sensitivity |
| Theorem proving (GPT-f, HTPS, AlphaProof, AlphaGeometry, Lean provers) | A tactic, an auxiliary construction, or a whole proof | Tactic states; AND-OR hypertrees; a geometry DSL | Formal kernel (exact) or symbolic closure | Best-first, PUCT, beam, pass@k with compiler-feedback refinement | GPT-f about 1k V100-hours per evaluation; AlphaProof about 180k TPU-days for autoformalization plus RL | Checker bugs exploited; misformalized statements proved |

**Program search puts the domain knowledge in a skeleton and searches only a small piece.** FunSearch's input is "an 'evaluate' function, an initial implementation of the function to evolve, which can be trivial, and potentially a skeleton". The authors justify the fixed skeleton because it "focuses the LLM resources on only evolving the critical part". The scope they state is **problems with an efficient evaluator, rich (non-binary) scoring feedback, and an isolable part to evolve**. Theorem proving is named as out of scope because it lacks a rich score ([FunSearch, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6)). The released evaluator rejects programs that call their own ancestors and discards failed runs ([evaluator.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/funsearch/main/implementation/evaluator.py)). AlphaEvolve generalizes the candidate to whole files and the evaluator to a dict of metrics with cascades of increasing difficulty. It reports that "optimizing for multiple metrics often improves results for the single target metric", and it names the need for an automated evaluator as its main limitation ([AlphaEvolve](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13131)). The follow-up on 67 math problems is the clearest warning in the field. Given arbitrary functions, the system "always eventually figured out a way to cheat" the numerical integration in the scorer, and **the fix was to restrict the representation** to bounded step functions. The same paper found that continuous scores beat discrete counts ([Georgiev, Gómez-Serrano, Tao, Wagner](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02864)). Prompt optimizers sit at the cheap, noisy end of this family. OPRO's fitness is accuracy on 3.5% of the GSM8K training set. Its authors concede that overfitting "is difficult to avoid", and adding one phrase to another dropped accuracy from 71.8 to 49.4 ([OPRO](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.03409)).

**Theorem proving is the limiting case: an exact, cheap terminal checker plus a learned proposal distribution.** GPT-f defines the state as a goal plus the proven theorems, the action as a theorem with a substitution, and the search as best-first over cumulative log-probability, later replaced by a learned value ([GPT-f](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.03393)). HTPS makes the AND structure explicit: the hyperedges are tactics whose subgoals must all be proved, and values multiply up the tree ([HTPS](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.11491)). AlphaProof frames proving as an RL problem over Lean tactic states with reward −1 per tactic. Its outer layer is a curriculum of about 80 million autoformalized statements chosen by "interestingness", and the training environment injects private axioms that a **final audit forbids in accepted proofs**. It cost about 100,000 TPU-days for autoformalization and 80,000 for RL, and each IMO 2024 solution took 2 to 3 days of test-time RL ([AlphaProof, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09833-y)). AlphaGeometry uses the symbolic engine as both data generator and goal test. The language model searches only the action space the engine cannot reach, namely auxiliary constructions, with beam 512 and depth 16. Its ablations to beam 8 or depth 2 still solved 21 of 30 against 25 at full settings ([AlphaGeometry, Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06747-5)). Since 2024 the Lean provers have moved to whole-proof generation with binary compiler reward and pass@k budgets up to 8,192 ([DeepSeek-Prover-V2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21801)).

**AutoML and NAS show that the representation, not the optimizer, carries the hypothesis.** ConfigSpace defines a space as typed hyperparameters (categorical, ordinal, numeric), an acyclic set of activation conditions, and forbidden clauses that blacklist combinations. Inactive children are stored as NaN and sampled by rejection, and SMAC's random forest imputes them out of range so that trees can split them off ([ConfigSpace guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/automl/ConfigSpace/main/docs/guide.md); [SMAC3 RF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/automl/SMAC3/main/smac/model/random_forest/abstract_random_forest.py)). ConfigSpace has **no subset type**, so subsets must be encoded, and the natural encoding is one bit per element with hypercube geometry, as in BOCS's {0,1}^d formulation and COMBO's product of complete graphs (nominal), path graphs (ordinal) and binary variables ([BOCS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08838); [COMBO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00448)). SMAC handles noisy evaluation over instances by racing challengers against incumbents on doubling instance counts ([SMAC3 intensifier](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/automl/SMAC3/main/smac/intensifier/intensifier.py)). Hyperband and BOHB add a fidelity knob, which is valid only if low-fidelity rankings agree with full ones ([Hyperband](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06560); [BOHB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01774)). NAS-Bench-101's 510 million encodings collapse to about 423,000 unique graphs once isomorphisms are removed ([Ying et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.09635)). The broader NAS record is sobering: "many NAS techniques struggle to significantly beat the average architecture baseline" ([Yang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12522)), and the DARTS space's 10^18 architectures have small performance variance ([White et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08727)).

**Quality-diversity and open-ended search change what the deliverable is.** MAP-Elites returns "the highest-performing solution at each point in the feature space" rather than one optimum ([Mouret and Clune](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909)). POET and its successors search over problems as well as solutions. They admit a new environment only if it passes a minimal criterion, a band filter on the score, "50 ≤ E_child(θ_child) ≤ 300" ([POET](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01753)). Enhanced POET characterizes an environment by **how it ranks a panel of agents** (PATA-EC), after clipping scores that are too low or too high ([Enhanced POET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08536)). OMNI-EPIC has an LLM write tasks and separate `get_success` checks as code, and it judges interestingness against an unbounded archive ([OMNI-EPIC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15568)). On the many-objective side, NSGA-II provably cannot cover the Pareto front in sub-exponential time once there are three or more objectives, because crowding distance treats objectives independently ([Zheng and Doerr](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13084)). NSGA-III with enough reference points recovers guarantees ([Wietheger and Doerr](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.08202)), and exact hypervolume contributions are #P-hard ([Bringmann and Friedrich](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.2636)).

**Classical formulations supply the exactness conditions the student's draft leans on.** Branch and bound returns a certified optimum only when branching partitions the space, the bounds are valid, and pruning compares against a feasible incumbent ([MILP B&B survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.06257v1)). With several objectives, each bound becomes a set (lower and upper bound sets, after Ehrgott and Gandibleux). A node is fathomed only when its whole lower bound set is dominated, and with three or more objectives bound sets "are more complex to compute" and splitting is harder ([Forget et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.05385)). Arc consistency prunes infeasible values soundly but cannot decide feasibility alone ([AC-3](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/aimacode/aima-pseudocode/master/md/AC-3.md)). Design of experiments offers the alternative to exhaustive search. Mixed-level orthogonal arrays such as OA(12, 2^4 3^1, 2), definitive screening designs in 2d + 1 runs, and resolution and strength choices all rest on **effect sparsity, hierarchy and heredity, which "may fail to hold in practice"** ([Woods and Lewis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.05248); [OA review](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15032v1)). Bayesian experimental design ranks designs by expected information gain about model parameters. For linear-Gaussian models the classical D-criterion does not depend on the unknown parameters ([Rainforth et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14545)).

**Benchmark search already exists, and its authors flag Goodharting themselves.** AutoBencher's candidate is a natural-language dataset description. It maximizes novelty + difficulty + 10·separability subject to a hard salience constraint, proposes descriptions from a (description, accuracy) history, re-ranks expensive criteria only at the end, and costs about $15 per run. Its authors worry that the search "may have discovered specific, adversarial interactions" between the generator and the test-taker, and they ran a human re-creation study, where the gains shrank ([AutoBencher](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.08351)). Adversarial filtering yields rankings that are "highly sensitive to the choice of adversary model" and over-selects items with low annotator agreement ([Phang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.08181)). IRT-based Fluid Benchmarking selects items by Fisher information a²p(1−p), reports higher validity with fifty times fewer items, and finds that **low-discrimination items are often mislabeled** ([Fluid Benchmarking](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.11106)).

## Failures concentrate where the evaluator is weaker than the search

The six families fail in the same few ways, and each failure has a direct counterpart in the student's draft.

**Optimizers find every hole in the checker.** HTPS reached 100% accuracy on an early Equations environment by proving 2 = 3, because NumPy evaluated two different double exponentials to 0.0. The fix was exact rational evaluation ([HTPS App. B.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.11491)). DeepSeek-Prover-V2's 7B model "solved" 13 PutnamBench problems its 671B sibling could not, by exploiting a Lean 4.9.0 `apply?` bug ([DeepSeek-Prover-V2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21801)). The AI Scientist edited its own time limit ([Lu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.06292)). In both prover cases the alarm was an implausible jump in success, and that heuristic transfers directly: if a cheap synthetic policy or a 2B model scores near π*, suspect the evaluator before believing the result.

**Specification errors pass verification untouched.** A sound kernel certifies "proof of this formal statement", not "solution of the intended problem". DeepSeek-Prover-V2 removed 2 of 49 PutnamBench solutions because the statements were misformulated, and miniF2F needed revisions for stray binders and wrong parenthesization ([DeepSeek-Prover-V2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.21801)). One solved miniF2F statement is "a much weaker version than the original problem" ([Polu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.01344)). AlphaGeometry's translated theorem dropped a betweenness condition ([AlphaGeometry](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06747-5)). The field's responses are worth copying. AlphaProof audits accepted proofs to the three standard Lean axioms. Goedel-Prover-V2 tries to prove the negation of unsolved statements to catch false ones ([Goedel-Prover-V2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03613)). Kimina uses unanimous multi-sample judges plus human monitoring ([Kimina](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11354)).

**Proxies Goodhart non-monotonically, and averaging does not save them.** Gao, Schulman and Hilton found that the gold reward first rises and then falls as optimization pressure grows, and that a KL penalty does not improve the frontier ([Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760)). Worst-case or uncertainty-penalized ensembles "practically eliminate" over-optimization, while mean ensembles still over-optimized under label noise ([Coste et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02743)). Skalse et al. prove that over all stochastic policies two rewards are unhackable only if one is constant. Non-trivial unhackable pairs exist only over restricted, for example finite, policy sets ([Skalse et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13085)). This is exactly why the student's B5 can only claim non-gameability relative to Π_cheap. LLM judges are especially weak: a null model wins 86.5% length-controlled on AlpacaEval 2.0, and paraphrasing the template does not help ([Zheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07137)). QDAIF saw reward hacking concentrate in the 0.995 to 1 band of its judge's scale ([QDAIF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13032)).

**The space, not the algorithm, decides the outcome, so random baselines are mandatory.** Lindauer and Hutter's NAS checklist requires comparison with random sampling (the space average) and random search (an anytime incumbent), "to assess whether good performance is due to a well-designed search space … or due to the NAS method" ([Lindauer and Hutter](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.02453)). Radosavovic et al. compare design spaces by the distribution of sampled members rather than by their best point ([Radosavovic et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13214)). Cheap evaluators must be validated by rank correlation against the full one. The best zero-cost proxy reached Spearman 0.82 on NAS-Bench-201 ([Abdelfattah et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.08134)), and low-fidelity rankings "can change dramatically" when the fidelity gap is large ([Elsken et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05377)).

**Many objectives break selection, and exactness needs valid bounds.** The literature backs four of the critique's points: NSGA-II's failure at three or more objectives, the extra difficulty of bound sets beyond two objectives, the weighted sum's blindness to non-convex regions ([Karl et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07438)), and the saturation of fixed-scale scores ([Rainbow Teaming](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.16822)). Together they support E5 and E8. A seven-component Pareto vector with bounds on two components prunes almost nothing. The established remedy is to demote components: validity becomes a minimal criterion, "what kind of design" becomes a behaviour descriptor, and only genuine trade-offs stay as objectives.

## Recasting the haggling benchmark in established formulations

Each family's formulation translates into a concrete change to the student's draft. The overall shape is a trusted kernel, a small typed space, a cascade evaluator, few objectives and staged search.

### Treat the game model as the kernel and declare its axioms

In theorem proving, everything rests on a kernel with declared axioms. AlphaProof's audit exists because the training environment injects private axioms that must never reach an accepted proof ([AlphaProof](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09833-y)). In the student's design, the analogous kernel is the bilateral-trade module that computes π*, U(·|x) and Δ*. The critique's E1 says this kernel silently depends on non-axis quantities: the prior μ, the cost support, p0, c, each δ_ι and the message likelihood. In formal-methods terms, the specification has free variables. The fix borrowed from FunSearch's skeleton is to write one fixed, trusted game module whose inputs are exactly the declared axes plus a named block of constants. It should compute π* with exact rational arithmetic (the HTPS lesson) and return explicit tie sets and walk-away outcomes rather than undefined values. Once it does, C6 is computed by the module from all of its true inputs, which repairs E9's locality failure, and C4's protection can be extended to every π*-based score (G7). G2, the cheap-talk problem, is a misformalization in the Goedel and Kimina sense: the module "proves" that a self-serving bare claim should move the buyer because the specification says so. The remedy is the same as in provers. Make information source and interest alignment an axis, set Δ* = 0 for self-serving bare assertions, and run a negation-style check: for each information item, assert in a unit test that the sign of Δ* matches the intended reading.

The scores themselves should follow FunSearch's rule that the evaluator is total. A run that produces no valid score is discarded, not scored arbitrarily. E2, E3 and E10 all come from scores that are undefined or misdefined on cells the space itself generates. Scoring in payoff space, reporting the walk-away rate separately, using v̄ in IR, and fixing a tie rule makes every score defined everywhere. Tao et al.'s finding that continuous scores guide search better than counts supports replacing the signed mean ratio Φ with a regression slope plus residual error, or a mean |ratio − 1| (G4) ([Tao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02864)).

### Encode the space as ConfigSpace would, then cut it to what can be scored

ConfigSpace's model of typed domains, an acyclic activation graph and forbidden clauses is the established way to represent the 22 axes, and it settles several critique items without any lattice theory. Nominal axes become categoricals and ordinal axes become ordinals; ordinals have two neighbours and categoricals k − 1, which is the locality distinction COMBO formalizes as path versus complete graphs. Each subset axis becomes one boolean per element, with a forbidden clause for emptiness. Distance on a subset is then Hamming on the bits, not Jaccard, which is exactly the critique's fix for E6, and hypercube geometry is what BOCS and COMBO use ([ConfigSpace forbiddens](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/automl/ConfigSpace/main/docs/reference/forbiddens.md); [COMBO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00448)). Axes that are meaningless under some setting become conditions rather than dummy levels. For example, if the 2×2 constraint C2 is ever relaxed, A15 credibility and the A13 pressure families should be conditional on the arms of A12 that use them rather than carry a dummy level, and the anchor policy's input should be marked inactive when the A9 line is absent (E13). E4's lattice claims and E7's group-action claims can simply be dropped, because no AutoML or NAS tool needs them. What NAS-Bench-101 does need is a canonical form that removes true duplicates, which here means designs that differ only on an inactive axis ([NAS-Bench-101](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.09635)). ConfigSpace also refuses a forbidden default configuration, a useful discipline for E12: the current gate should be expressible as a legal configuration, which forces adding level 2 to A16 and a control/baseline axis.

ConfigSpace samples by rejection, so a legal region of 12.5% of the item space (the critique's verified count) is tolerable for sampling. The real problem is size, and NAS and RegNet point to the answer: shrink the space to the hypothesis being tested ([RegNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13678)). Constraining A18 = 1 for v0.1 (G1) removes the undefined multi-turn π*. C1, C2 and C7 then force A2, A12 and A11, so those are constants, not axes (E13). Names, scene openings and option order become nuisance factors counterbalanced inside each design, not coordinates (E7, B3 in G5). One more consequence follows from the critique's observation that synthetic policies act on annotations, not text. The wording axes (A1, A4, A9, A10, and the pressure family within A13) are invisible to the synthetic evaluator, just as AlphaGeometry2's DSL could not express six IMO problems and so no search could solve them ([AlphaGeometry2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03544)). On my count, the game-structure axes the synthetic stage can see (A3, A5, A6, A7, A8, nonempty A14, A15, A19 with one turn) span 453,600 combinations before constraints, or about 2.9 × 10^7 if the pressure-family subset is kept. Synthetic scoring on annotations is cheap, so this stage can be **enumerated exhaustively**, with no beam and no bounds. Wording axes have to be chosen on real models, which is a different and much smaller experiment.

### Turn the axioms into a cascade of minimal criteria and widen the adversaries

AlphaEvolve's evaluation cascade and POET's minimal criterion together give the right evaluator shape. Stage 0 is static and exact: feasibility, a total π*, at least one decision-relevant information item, at least one Δ* = 0 control and a length-matched neutral message (G3). Stage 1 runs synthetic policies. Here the critique's G5 and G6 are the leaky-verifier problem from program search. Policies that read annotations make B3 trivially true and B6 equal to 1, just as a discretized positivity check let AlphaEvolve cheat. The remedy follows Reasoning Gym's principle that verifiers must be algorithmic and the solution space large enough to resist hacking ([Reasoning Gym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24760)). Add text-level synthetic buyers that parse noisy paraphrases. Widen Π_bad with "ignore every message", the over-updater and "right direction, fixed step of 1". Score each design by the **minimum** gap over this panel, following Coste et al.'s conservative aggregation. The minimum also matches the critique's corrected F1 and F3 definitions (E11). Stage 2 runs real calibration models. B1, B2 and B5 become pass/fail gates, as AutoBencher treats salience, rather than terms traded against cost. The steering use case adds a band filter in POET's sense: baseline yielding must sit above its floor, and π* must sit away from the grid ceiling, so that steering has headroom in both directions.

Two further guards come from the benchmark literature. Fluid Benchmarking's finding that low-discrimination items are often mislabeled gives a cheap annotation audit: on real calibration models, item families whose responses are non-monotone in the intended pressure or information direction are probably mis-annotated ([Fluid Benchmarking](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.11106)). This bears directly on the student's own weakness 3, the ambiguity of "another buyer offered 4". Separately, the critique's G10 (F7 rewards verbatim Concordia) is an instance of BetterBench's and Bean et al.'s warning that checklist items belong in review gates, not in optimized scores. Provenance should be reported, not maximized ([Bean et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04703)).

### Collapse seven objectives into one information criterion plus thresholds

Discrimination (F1), construct separation (F3) and power (F5) are three proxies for one question: how precisely does a design identify a buyer's yielding and updating? Bayesian experimental design answers that question directly. Parameterize the synthetic buyer as π* shifted by λ under pressure and scaled by an update weight w on Δ*, plus noise. Then score a design by its expected information gain about (λ, w), or, in the linear-Gaussian case, by the D-criterion, which needs no prior guess of the parameters ([Rainforth et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14545); [Stallrich et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13943)). This is my inference rather than a published recipe, but it has three useful properties. It rewards the opposed cell, where pressure and information push in opposite directions, because crossing the two factors is what makes λ and w separately estimable; the critique flags that cell as unscored. It makes power a derived quantity instead of a separate objective. And it reduces the Pareto problem to information versus cost, a two-objective front on which exact multi-objective branch and bound with bound sets is tractable if it is wanted ([Parragh and Tricoire](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.06823)). Reliability (F4) belongs as a threshold measured on real models. Gaming margin (F2) belongs as the B5 gate. If the student wants to see variety rather than one winner, MAP-Elites with a PATA-EC-style descriptor (the clipped rank vector a design induces over the synthetic and calibration panel) yields an archive of designs that order buyers differently. That is more informative than a seven-dimensional front, which the critique correctly predicts will be enormous.

### Run a staged search with held-out gold checks

The search that fits this formulation has three stages, each matched to its evaluator. Stage A enumerates the synthetic-visible space exhaustively and reports the full distribution of the information criterion alongside the winners, since a narrow distribution means design choices barely matter (the Yang et al. lesson). Stage B handles the axes only real models can see: wording, readout, option order, a relabelled or shifted price grid to test digit priors (G9), and paraphrase and context counts. It uses a mixed-level orthogonal array or a definitive screening design over the stage-A finalists, for example 21 runs for ten three-level factors. Resolution IV or strength 3 is the safer choice if wording × readout interactions are plausible, since the critique's weakness 9 is exactly an interaction worry. If more adaptivity is wanted, SMAC's random forest handles categorical and conditional inputs natively, and its racing over instances suits noisy log-probability readouts. Paraphrase or context count can serve as a Hyperband fidelity only after its rank correlation with full evaluation has been checked. Stage C validates on held-out models disjoint from the calibration models, logs the proxy-versus-held-out gap, and picks at the held-out peak, Gao's curve used as early stopping. Every stage reports random-sampling and random-search baselines. If a beam is kept anywhere, the search must be labelled heuristic, which resolves E8 by honesty rather than by proof.

The open-world extension in section 12 of the draft is where LLM-guided search fits. OMNI-EPIC, ACD and AutoBencher all show LLMs proposing new task families against an archive, with novelty judged relative to nearest neighbours ([ACD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.07577)). New pressure or information message families can be proposed this way, but each proposal must arrive with the annotation the game module needs (ι, ψ, δ_ι, source), must pass a separate label-checker plus human spot checks as in Perez et al. ([Perez et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09251)), and must never see the scoring code. The critique's G11 caveat holds: old scores survive an extension, but archive or Pareto status must be recomputed.

## Where the analogies stop

Several borrowed ideas do not transfer, and three uncertainties remain. LLM-guided program search is the wrong tool for the core of this problem. FunSearch and AlphaEvolve earn their cost when candidates are open-ended code and the evaluator is cheap and exact. Here the candidates are a few hundred thousand typed tuples that a laptop can enumerate, so an LLM proposer adds exploit risk (AI Scientist, ImpossibleBench ([ImpossibleBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20270))) without adding coverage. AlphaProof's compute scale is irrelevant for the same reason. What transfers from theorem proving is the separation between a trusted kernel and an untrusted proposer, and the habit of auditing the specification.

The analogy to an exact checker also breaks at a deeper level. A Lean kernel's verdict is not a modelling choice, but π* is: even a fully declared game module encodes one normative theory of bilateral trade. The critique's E1 and G2 cannot be fully solved by better engineering. They can be made explicit, and scores can be split into prior-robust predictions (sign of Δ*, pressure invariance, IR) and prior-dependent ones (regret, fidelity magnitude). That split is the closest analogue to AlphaProof reporting results "assuming the soundness of the Lean kernel and the declared axioms".

The uncertainties are real. No source in these notes searches negotiation or multi-agent benchmark designs for construct validity against synthetic agents with known trait levels; the closest are AutoBencher, ACD and IRT item selection. The transfer of Gao's over-optimization curve from RLHF to design search is an inference that no study has measured. And the single-turn restriction (G1) conflicts with Pan et al.'s evidence that static evaluations miss feedback-loop effects ([Pan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06627)). Multi-turn designs should therefore return as a conservative extension once the single-decision kernel is sound, not be dropped for good.

## Conclusion

The surveyed literature reframes the student's question. The hard part of "searching for the best benchmark design" is not the search: once the space is cut to what the evaluator can see, exhaustive enumeration costs minutes, and the NAS record shows that clever optimizers barely beat random sampling in a well-designed space. The hard part is making the evaluator deserve trust. That means a game kernel with declared constants, scores defined on every cell, synthetic adversaries strong enough to fail bad designs, and real-model checks held out from selection. Most of the draft's mathematical apparatus (lattices, quotients, Pareto-width bounds) can go, and almost nothing that matters is lost.

One reframing is genuinely new for this project. Discrimination, separation and power are one quantity, information about a buyer's yielding and updating parameters, so the benchmark can be designed the way experimentalists design experiments: by an information criterion over a crossed pressure × information layout, screened on a handful of real models. That also aligns the benchmark with the later steering work. A design chosen to estimate λ and w precisely is the design that will measure whether a steering vector moves λ without moving w, which is the separation the study exists to test.
